# Questions, Answers, and Solvable Structure

This group of essays unfolds around one main thread:

> An answer gives a result inside an already specified problem, whereas questioning constructs the solvable structure of the problem itself.

The whole series advances in the order of "phenomenon -> method -> algorithm -> mathematics -> meta-problem."

## Core Claims Of The Series

- Questioning and answering are not the same cognitive activity.
- The quality of a question determines the ceiling of its answer.
- A good question is not just a sentence with a question mark; it is a solvable structure.
- From an algorithmic perspective, questioning organizes search while answering compresses results.
- From an MDP perspective, a good question is equivalent to a good state abstraction.
- For fixed control problems, the mathematical criterion of a good question is minimal sufficiency plus closure under control.
- For robustness across environments, causal stability is required on top of that.

## Writing Notes

- Keep formulas light in the first four essays; get the intuition and the argumentative line through first.
- Introduce MDP from essay five onward, but still keep explanatory language in the foreground.
- Essays six and seven can use formal definitions, theorems, and counterexamples.
- Essay eight returns to the meta level, but it should not dissolve into vague philosophy; it should stay centered on the operation of problem construction.
